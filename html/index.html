6 - 10 October 2009

Exhibition celebrating 300 Years of Papermaking

in Penicuik Town Hall, 33 High Street, Penicuik, EH26 8HS

Papermaking in Midlothian dates from 1709. The first mill on the Esk was established by the ‘Queen’s Printer’, Agnes Campbell, who leased land at Valleyfield from Sir John Clerk of Penicuik, with the right to build a paper mill and to extract water from St Mungo’s Well. Within 100 years, ten mills were established between Penicuik and Lasswade, mostly producing fine quality papers.

The industry became vital to the development of the area both economically and socially. It is now 40 years since Eskmills closed and 33 years since Valleyfield and Pomathorn Mills closed. Dalmore, the last mill on the Esk, ceased production in 2004.

From 6–10 October, there will be an exhibition in the Town Hall looking at Penicuik’s history as ‘The Papermaking Town’, with an events programme to include demonstrations of hand papermaking, guided walks, activities for schools and other events. A travelling exhibition on papermaking in the area will be displayed in libraries throughout Midlothian until February 2010.

The exhibitions are part of a wider project to highlight and celebrate the history of papermaking in the area. Volunteers are encouraged to come forward to get involved in finding out more about the origins and development of the industry. Another part of the project is recording the memories of those who worked in the industry.

The project is led by the Penicuik Historical Society, and is funded by the Heritage Lottery Fund with the support of Midlothian Council.
